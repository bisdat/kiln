# Kiln 0.2.0-dev.29.18

This patch brings Kiln in Simplified Chinese and Kiln's own graphics tools: a frame cap, DLSS settings and a readout. Behind the game's FSR 3 option, Kiln can now run AMD FSR 4, FSR 3.1 or Intel XeSS. The launcher gets Kiln's Discord (Send to Kiln support, your posts and their replies, Rich Presence), all of it optional. Kiln's overlay steps aside for OptiScaler, and the game no longer dies when the overlay cannot draw. ShroudForge's startup pass keeps going when one mod fails. EMF's Lua mods come in with EMF's download, mod settings stick in copy mode and on a Steam Deck, and the launcher offers Repair when an antivirus removed one of Kiln's files. A new SDK (mod API 0.2.10) brings Kiln tasks for a mod's heavy work off the game thread, the game's own texts in the player's language, and wraps in the prediction storage. Everything else is as in 0.2.0-dev.29.17.

**Not yet checked in a real game.** This patch passed Kiln's tests, lab checks on a dedicated server and the release's own checks on a trial build. What a game client shows has not been seen in a real game yet: the graphics tools and the upscalers behind FSR 3, Kiln's overlay next to OptiScaler, Kiln in Chinese in the game, the game's texts for mods, Kiln tasks and their world reads and writes, on a dedicated server or a game client, wraps in the prediction storage and EMF's Lua mods. Kiln's own steps at the game's exit and the launcher's antivirus messages have not run on a Windows PC yet, and the launcher's Discord features have not yet been used with the live relay on the Kiln Discord. If something looks or works wrong, tell us on GitHub or Discord with `kiln/logs/kiln.log`.

## Players

- **Kiln in Simplified Chinese, translated by XiaoHuLi666 (XHL).** Kiln's first language besides English: the whole launcher and all of Kiln's own texts in the game. In the launcher, choose 简体中文 in Settings, Language, or on a Steam Deck in the new Language row of Settings. "System" (the default) picks it when your system's language is Chinese, and dates and numbers follow it. In the game, choose it in Mods > Kiln settings > Language, or in the launcher's "Kiln in game". "Same as the game" (the default) shows Kiln in Chinese when the game is in Chinese. The overlay draws Chinese with the game's own Chinese font (Kiln ships no font). Until that font is loaded, or if it cannot be read, Kiln's texts in the overlay stay English rather than boxes, and the Language entry lists Chinese as `zh-CN`. Mods' own texts follow the game's language as before, and messages that come from Kiln itself in the launcher (errors, task names) are still English. Not yet checked in a real game. Thank you, XiaoHuLi666! To translate Kiln into your language: [translating.md](../translating.md). For translators, the check script (`bun scripts/i18n-check.ts`) and Kiln's tests now also refuse number formats that differ from English, markup, links, invisible characters and confirm words with a space.
- **Kiln's own graphics tools: a frame cap, DLSS settings and a readout.** The Kiln menu (Insert) has a new **Graphics** tab, and the launcher's Settings page a **Graphics** card. They do what players used extra mods for. A frame cap paces the frames the game renders, from 20 to 500 a second: the finished frame is shown at once, and the game starts its next one on time. You can pick the DLSS render preset (J, K, L or M, the ones the game's DLSS has) and the DLSS render size (DLSS's own, one scale for each DLSS mode, or one scale for every mode). DLAA stays as the game has it. A readout in the top right corner shows the frames rendered and shown a second, the frame time, the upscaler, its mode, render and output size, and the DLSS preset. Everything is off until you turn it on, and nothing changes for anyone who does not. The cap and the readout apply at once. DLSS settings apply when the game next sets DLSS up: a world load, or a DLSS or resolution change in the game's settings. A DLSS setting turned on for the first time applies from the next game start. Kiln's frame cap and DLSS settings step aside on their own when OptiScaler or a DLSS frame generation mod (Streamline, `EnshroudedVKFG.ini`) is installed. With DLSSTweaks or another DLSS loader, only the DLSS settings step aside. The Graphics page says why. Not yet checked in a real game. Details: [graphics.md](../graphics.md).
- **A better upscaler behind the game's FSR 3: AMD FSR 4, FSR 3.1 or Intel XeSS.** Choose FSR 3 as the upscaler in the game's graphics settings, then choose what Kiln runs in its place: on the Kiln menu's **Graphics** tab (Insert), on the launcher's Settings > **Graphics** card, or in the Deck's Settings. **AMD FSR** runs FSR 4 where your graphics card and driver run it (AMD's own choice: RX 9000, RX 7000, RX 6000 and recent AMD handhelds), else FSR 3.1. You can keep it on FSR 3.1. **Intel XeSS** runs on Intel, AMD and NVIDIA cards. The default is the game's own FSR 3, and nothing changes until you choose another. A change applies when the game next sets FSR up (a world load, or a change of FSR's quality, the resolution or the 3D render scale). The first choice while the game runs applies from the next game start. Kiln shows what runs now and, when your choice does not run, why (files not downloaded, a missing graphics feature, another upscaler tool installed, and so on). The launcher shows the same while the game runs, and what ran in your last game after it closes. Kiln steps aside on its own next to OptiScaler, frame generation mods and other upscaler tools' files in the game folder. Upscaling only: no frame generation yet. Not yet checked in a real game. We are looking for testers with AMD, Intel and NVIDIA cards and the Steam Deck. Details: [graphics.md](../graphics.md#behind-the-games-fsr-3-phase-b).
- **The upscaler files are no longer a preview.** AMD's and Intel's files download in the launcher's Settings > Graphics card, under the upscaler choice, and in the Deck's Settings. When you choose AMD FSR or Intel XeSS without its files, the launcher offers the download right there. Before a vendor's first download you see its licence in full (on the Deck the right stick scrolls it) and agree to it. Then you see the progress, and Remove takes the files out again. Kiln checks every file against the size and SHA-256 it pinned, and the game checks them again before it loads them. The files go into the game's `kiln/vendor` folder, never next to the game. The `"preview": ["upscalers"]` setting is no longer needed. Details: [graphics.md](../graphics.md#upscaler-files).
- **Linux: the launcher says when its window cannot start, instead of leaving it empty.** When the launcher's page has not come up 15 seconds after the window opened, and the launcher has nothing left to try by itself (it already draws without the GPU, or you asked for a mode), a message now says what it tried and where launcher.log is. It also offers what you can still try: "Start without the GPU" (when it drew with it), "Start without WebKit's sandbox" (when WebKit runs it), "Open the log folder", "Quit". The cause of an empty window is often not known, so each button is a try. The message is the system's own (GTK, or zenity, kdialog or a desktop notice when GTK cannot show it), not part of the launcher's page. Details: [app.md](../launcher/app.md#webkits-sandbox-linux).
- **Linux: WebKit's sandbox, your choice.** The launcher checks at each start whether the sandbox WebKit would use (bubblewrap, `bwrap`) can start on your system. In a normal start WebKit does not use it for the launcher: WebKitGTK's GTK 3 version uses it only when `WEBKIT_FORCE_SANDBOX=1` is set. So this changes nothing for most players, and it does not explain an empty window on Ubuntu 24.04 and systems built on it. When WebKit would use it and it cannot start (where WebKit would end the launcher at once), a message before the window offers "Turn the sandbox off and start" (where the launcher can), "Start anyway" or "Quit", and names the proper fix. The launcher never turns the sandbox off by itself. Settings > Appearance > "WebKit's sandbox" turns it off or back on for the next start. `--no-webkit-sandbox` or `--webkit-sandbox`, and `KILN_LAUNCHER_WEBKIT_SANDBOX=off|on` do it for one start. launcher.log has `sandbox:` lines and the support report a `WebKit sandbox:` line.
- **Linux: "Draw the launcher without the GPU" works at the start again.** Since 0.2.0-dev.29 the launcher did not read that setting when it started: a change for test builds held its data folder until after the window was made. So a launcher that had fallen back to drawing without the GPU started with the GPU again each time, and its first log lines (how it draws, the WebKitGTK version) only went to the terminal. Both work again. If the setting is on for you (you turned it on, or the launcher did after a start whose window stayed black), the launcher now draws without the GPU at every start, also where it drew with the GPU in the versions before. Turn it off in Settings > Appearance to draw with the GPU again. The switch's text and launcher.log say when this is why.
- **The launcher no longer lags on every page but Play.** On some Linux PCs a page took about a second to open and typing trailed behind by up to a second, with or without the GPU. The flicker of the fire and the soft light that follows the pointer made the launcher draw the whole page again many times a second. They now draw only their own small layers, so the launcher is much lighter on every page but Play, and the look is the same. The light stays still while the fire is off (Settings > Appearance > Animated fire), while the launcher draws without the GPU, and when your system asks for reduced motion. With reduced motion the fire no longer flickers either, also when you turn it on while the launcher runs (on KDE, animation speed set to Instant). While the launcher draws without the GPU, the flames of lit mods stand still too, as each flicker made the computer draw them again.
- **Kiln and OptiScaler.** Kiln now recognises [OptiScaler](https://github.com/optiscaler/OptiScaler) (the upscaler and frame generation tool) in the game folder under any of its names. With OptiScaler's own in-game menu on (its default), or its frame generation set, Kiln's overlay steps aside on its own, as it does for NVIDIA Streamline: Kiln's key bindings, the Mods entry and mods' menu rows keep working, and the Mods entry says why ("OptiScaler's own overlay menu is on", or "OptiScaler's frame generation is on"). Before, both overlays hooked the same graphics calls and both menus opened with Insert, and Kiln's menu could stay missing. To have Kiln's overlay with OptiScaler, set `OverlayMenu=false` under `[Menu]` in `OptiScaler.ini` and leave `FGInput` and `FGOutput` under `[FrameGen]` at `auto`. OptiScaler's own menu and frame generation are then off, and its upscaling keeps working. Kiln then draws as it does without OptiScaler. OptiScaler's nightly builds since October 2026 run frame generation with their menu off too, so turning the menu off alone is not enough. `KILN_UI_OVERLAY=1` still tries both at once. The launcher's Play page names OptiScaler and says what to expect, the support report includes `OptiScaler.ini`, and an OptiScaler package added to the Library is left out as a tool that runs on its own. Not yet checked on a PC with OptiScaler. Details: [ui.md](../ui.md#optiscaler).
- **The game no longer dies when Kiln's overlay cannot draw.** A player's game closed right after the main menu showed, with OptiScaler's frame generation on and its menu off. Kiln's overlay work failed, and the game died right after. Most likely Kiln drew on the queue that OptiScaler's frame generation presents from, which cannot take Kiln's work. Now Kiln draws only on the game's own queues, and only on one that can draw. On a queue of frame generation's it draws nothing and lets every frame through as the game made it. Without such a tool nothing changes: Kiln draws where it drew before. Any other failure of Kiln's overlay (its graphics work, a fault) now does the same: Kiln stops drawing for the rest of that game session and hands the game's frames on untouched. kiln.log says it once, with the reason. The Mods entry shows the reason, and nothing of Kiln's keeps your keyboard or mouse. Key bindings and mods' menu rows keep working. The next start tries the overlay again. Not yet checked on a PC with OptiScaler. Details: [ui.md](../ui.md#when-the-overlay-fails-during-a-session).
- **A switch for Kiln's overlay in the launcher.** Settings, the Kiln card, "Overlay": `off` turns Kiln's overlay drawing off, the same as `KILN_UI_OVERLAY=0`. Steam on Windows cannot set that variable for the game, so this is the way there. It is stored as `"ui": {"overlay": "off"}` in `kiln/kiln.json` and applies from the next game start. `auto`, the default, changes nothing. Key bindings, the Mods entry and mods' menu rows keep working with the overlay off. Details: [ui.md](../ui.md#frame-generation-mods-and-drawing-off).
- **The overlay's crash guard watches a little longer.** Since 0.2.0-dev.29 a game that closes while Kiln's overlay is starting makes the next start leave the overlay's drawing off. It now also counts a game that closes during the overlay's first frames (60 of them, or 20 seconds), and one that closes within 30 seconds after the overlay's own work failed and it turned itself off. A normal quit does not count. Kiln sees one on Linux and the Steam Deck, and from this release on it looks for one on Windows too, but that the game's own quit on Windows gets there is not confirmed yet: there a quit during those first frames (about a second at 60 frames a second), or in those 30 seconds, may still cost one start without the overlay. The start after a start without the overlay always tries it again, however long that start ran and however it ended. Before, that start had to run a minute: a quicker quit, from the main menu say, kept the overlay's drawing off at every later start until `kiln/state/overlay-start.guard` was deleted. Such a file gets its new try at the first start of this release. A second close at the same point of the overlay's start still keeps the drawing off until that file is deleted. Details: [ui.md](../ui.md#overlay-startup-crash-guard).
- **One ShroudForge startup mod that fails no longer costs you the others.** ShroudForge writes its startup mods (the ones that change game data) into the game files in one pass before the game starts. When one of them failed there, for example because another mod had changed the same data, ShroudForge put the game data back and the game started with none of them. The ShroudForge that comes with Kiln now runs that pass once more without the mod that failed (and without mods that need it), so the others load. kiln.log names the mod and its error, and the launcher (Mods > Engines, the Deck's Home) and the support report warn with its name and error and suggest turning it off or checking for a clash with another mod. It tries again only once, and not for failures that are not one mod's (access denied, the game's files). Details: [shroudforge.md](../shroudforge.md).
- **A Kiln mod unzipped into EML's `mods` folder now says so.** Kiln's EML support listed such files as "? skipped: no mod.json (not an EML mod)". It now says it is a Kiln mod and to add its zip in the launcher (Library, Add mods, or on a server, unzip it into `kiln/mods/<mod>/`), and logs that as a warning. This covers a Kiln mod's folder or zip there, and one unzipped loose. Other zips without a `mod.json` now say so instead of "specified file not found in archive".
- **EMF's Lua mods: added from EMF's download, and no longer "Can't run".** Adding EMF's zip in the launcher left out every Lua mod in it (Beastmaster, Bounty Board, Wandering Champion, Grave Keeper, Hand-over, Kill Counter, Life Skills, Minimap, Necromancer) as "not a recognised mod package". Kiln now adds them as EMF mods, beside the DLL mods it already added. EMF's lua mod reads Lua mods only in `emf/mods`, so Kiln copies each one you switch on there. The Lua examples and the starter in EMF's download are left out, as EMF does not install them either. Lua mods you put into `emf/mods` yourself now show as loading instead of "Can't run". When EMF's lua mod is missing or switched off, the launcher says that instead. EMF's own files in its download (its core, its builder of EML content and its frame-time recorder) are no longer added as DLL mods, which Kiln would have loaded at every start: they are part of EMF, and Kiln says so. Not yet checked in a real game. Details: [emf.md](../emf.md#install-a-mod).
- **No more "One Steam setting" banner where it is not needed, by bisdat.** On Linux with GE-Proton 11-11 or newer chosen for Enshrouded in Steam (for the game itself, or as Steam's default for all games), Proton loads Kiln by itself, but the launcher's Play page and the Deck's Help still asked for the `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="dinput8=n,b" %command%` launch option. The launcher now reads which Proton Steam uses for the game and leaves the advice out there. Older and unknown Proton builds keep it, and if a game start really runs without Kiln ("No Kiln"), the launch option is offered again as the fix, whatever the Proton. The separate advice for Ultimate ASI Loader (`xinput9_1_0`) is unchanged. (GitHub #77, pull request #78.)
- **A ShroudForge mod you switched off no longer runs through XHL-Core.** Some ShroudForge mods are XHL-Core plugins too, such as Building Tools for ShroudForge. When a profile had one off, or did not have it at all, Kiln switched it off for ShroudForge but its folder stayed in the game's `mods` folder, and XHL-Core still loaded its DLL from there. Without its ShroudForge side, the game could crash at every start. While such a mod is off, Kiln now renames its `native-plugin.ini` to `native-plugin.ini.kiln-off`, so XHL-Core skips the folder, and renames it back when you switch the mod on. Settings the mod keeps in that file stay as they are, also through an update of the mod. Kiln does this only in the copies it placed itself, never in a folder you put there yourself, and also when the mod's version is no longer in the Library. The old notice that such a mod "may still run from there" is gone. Where the game has XHL-Core, a note says once what Kiln renamed.
- **Play waits while a profile is being applied.** Loading a profile, adding a mod, installing a modpack or updating Kiln writes your mods into the game folder, which can take a while. Play could be pressed in the middle of it and was refused only after the click, or not at all when the work ran in the background (a drop, a modpack, Kiln's install with your profile). Play now says "Applying profile…" (or "Installing Kiln…" and "Removing Kiln…" while Kiln itself goes in or out) and stays off until the work is done or has failed, whatever started it, on the desktop and on the Steam Deck. The other ways to start the game, such as Without mods, wait too.
- **Stop waiting when Steam does not start the game.** After Play, the launcher waited up to 5 minutes for a game Steam never started, for example when you cancelled the launch while Steam was processing Vulkan shaders, and Play stayed off all that time. While Play waits for the game, the Play page and the Steam Deck's Home now offer **Stop waiting**, and Play is back at once. It is gone once the game runs, and it never closes a game.
- **"Updated" when you add a newer file of a mod you have.** Dropping a newer version of a mod that is already in your mod list said "Added {name}". It now says "Updated {name}" with both versions, for example "Updated Night Owl: 1.2.0 → 1.3.0", on the desktop and on the Deck's Add mods. A new mod still says "Added", and the same version again says what it said before.
- **Repair for Kiln files your antivirus removed.** When a file Kiln installed is gone from the game folder, for example a DLL an antivirus deleted or put in quarantine, the Runtime page now says which ("1 of Kiln's files is missing: kiln\plugins\kiln-greeneye.dll") and offers **Repair**, which installs the same Kiln version again and puts the missing files back. Before, it counted such a file as "Changed" and offered nothing. When a missing file is a DLL, the banner says an antivirus may have removed it and links the new Antivirus section of the docs: which files Kiln puts in the game folder, why antivirus programs flag them, how to add an exclusion for the game folder (Windows Security and others), how to restore a file from quarantine, then Repair. Files that are there but changed keep the "Changed" banner as before. Details: [app.md](../launcher/app.md#antivirus).
- **The launcher says when an antivirus blocks Kiln's install.** When Windows did not let Kiln write one of its DLLs or exes during an install, update or Repair, the launcher showed only "Access is denied. (os error 5)", and the install was undone. The usual cause is an antivirus that holds the file or moves it to quarantine. The Runtime page, Play and the launcher's message now say "Windows did not let Kiln write kiln\plugins\kiln-greeneye.dll. An antivirus or another program may be blocking it." They say what to do and link the Antivirus section of the docs. The error itself is still there, under Details. The support report says it too, also after the launcher was closed. A file the game holds is not called an antivirus: Kiln asks you to quit the game. On Windows the launcher also says when its own uninstall.exe, or its exe, is gone from its folder. Details: [app.md](../launcher/app.md#antivirus).
- **The support report has XHL-Core's own log.** When XHL-Core is installed, the report now includes its `XHL-Native-Plugin-Host.log`, from the game folder or from Kiln's copy of XHL-Core, with your paths and names taken out as in Kiln's own logs. It shows which XHL plugins XHL-Core loaded, skipped or refused.
- **ShroudForge mod settings changed in the game now apply on a Steam Deck with the game on an SD card.** There the launcher often runs the game in "copy" path mode, and the game's Kiln menu (MODS) changed a second copy of the mod's settings that ShroudForge never reads: the values looked saved, the game started with the old ones, and the next profile update put that copy back as it came. The menu now shows and changes the copy ShroudForge runs (its FOLDER line names `mods\<mod>`), and profile updates keep what you set, as they do for the launcher's own settings editor. A copy that an older Kiln changed that way is kept in `kiln/backups/` at the next profile update, and the launcher says so. On a Deck, change a ShroudForge mod's settings in the game's Kiln menu, or in the launcher's desktop screens (Settings, Desktop UI, then Mods, the mod, Settings, with the game closed): Deck mode itself has only on, off and remove for mods so far. Not yet checked on a Deck. Thanks to ljam for the report and the photos.
- **A changed mod copy is never thrown away without a word.** In copy path mode, a profile update replaced Kiln's copy of a mod under `kiln/profiles/` silently when it had been changed in the game folder. A copy whose only change is the mod's own settings file (an EML mod's `config.txt`, say) now stays as it is. Any other changed copy is moved to `kiln/backups/` before the Library's files go in, and the launcher's notes name it.
- **Mod files are never read-only any more.** A mod added to the Library from a folder kept a read-only file read-only, and so did the copy placed in the game and a folder of yours that Kiln took over, so a mod's or your settings could not be saved there. Kiln now makes those files writable for you (only that: no other permission changes), and the next profile update fixes copies placed before. A zip was never affected.
- **ShroudForge's errors and warnings count in kiln.log.** Kiln showed every ShroudForge line in kiln.log as information, so the support report's warning and error counts and the launcher's log filters left ShroudForge's own errors out. They now keep their level.
- **A ShroudForge mod with a `native-plugin.ini` is listed once.** The game's Mods page listed such a mod (Unified Tweaks) under ShroudForge and again under XHL as "Not loaded". ShroudForge's own DLL loader loads it, so it is now one row under ShroudForge. XHL-Core's own mods stay under XHL.
- **A server's ShroudForge mods (and other engines' mods) keep one hash while they run.** A server told joining players a hash of the mod's whole folder, with its settings and the files the mod writes there, so it changed every minute: the launcher never found the mod in a player's Library, and a modpack built from the server stopped matching a minute later. Kiln's copy of a mod is now named by its Library entry. A server's modpack still carries the mods with the owner's settings, as before. **Players:** if the launcher refuses a server's modpack with "the modpack lacks what the server requires", update the Kiln launcher to this version. Joining a server never checked these mods and still does not. **Server owners:** your modpack does not need building again for this update. Players who have not updated the launcher need to update it. When this launcher refuses a modpack, its message now says what to do: update the launcher, and if it is up to date, ask the server's owner to build the modpack again.
- **Kiln's Discord in the launcher, all of it optional.** A new **Community** page (after Servers) and a **Community** tab on the Deck (between Editor and Help) bring the Kiln Discord into the launcher. **Send to Kiln support** writes the support post for you from a support report: you write one line (on the Deck you pick what went wrong and when), the launcher shows exactly what will be posted and which files go, and the post appears in #support on the Kiln Discord. Your logs go to the Kiln team only, unless you choose to attach them to the post or not to send them. It is also offered on the Logs page, next to Play's problem banners (after the fix they already suggest) and on a new notice when the game closed while Kiln wrote a crash report. When a report matches a known problem, its fix shows first, and you can still send. ## Mod authors

- **The mod API is 0.2.10.** Everything new is additive: no public member of 0.2.0-dev.29.17 was removed or changed. Use the 0.2.0-dev.29.18 SDK and MDK. New in it: the game's own texts, Kiln tasks and their world reads and writes (experimental, KILNX003), wraps in the prediction storage, and `TryReadWritable` / `TryGetWritable`.
- **The game's own texts in the player's language: `Content.Text` and `Content.TooltipValues`** (mod API 0.2.10). On a game client, `Content.Text` gives the text the game shows for a loca tag, in the language the game shows now: a tooltip row's label, an item's name, any text the game has. `Content.TextAvailable` says whether it works here, and `Content.TextLanguage` names the language. It runs on the game client's main thread only. On a dedicated server it returns null (`Texts.Get` stays the server's way, English only, from a catalog). `Content.TooltipValues(ItemId)` gives the rows of an item type's tooltip with their labels and numbers, and `GameDataEntry.TooltipValues` does the same for a perk, a buff or a skill node. Native plugins get `text` and `text_info` in kiln_content.h. Not yet checked in a real game. Details: [services/content.md](../services/content.md#the-games-own-texts).
- **Kiln tasks: a mod's heavy work off the game thread (GitHub #45, pull request #89).** `Context.Tasks.Run(name, work)` runs the work on Kiln's own worker threads and returns a `KilnTask` (`KilnTask<T>` with a result) at once. Use it for a search over many entities, path finding, a big calculation, or file and network work, and hand the result back to the tick in small pieces. `KilnTaskOptions` gives work that runs for seconds a thread of its own (`LongRunning`) and orders a mod's own queued tasks (`Priority`). A mod that runs tasks puts `<KilnFeatures>tasks.pool</KilnFeatures>` in its project, so Kiln loads it only where the task pool runs (capability `tasks.pool`). Without it the analyzer KILN121 warns. Tasks run on a dedicated server and on a game client. A task goes back to the world it was started in: from client code (the game's main thread) that is the client world, and `ctx.ToTick(WorldKind.Client)` or `ToTick(WorldKind.Server)` names a world. `KilnTask.Wait()` is refused on the tick and on the game's main thread: await the task instead. Native plugins get `kiln_tasks.h` v1. The sample `FetchNearby` brings items from the containers near a player, with the search in a task. Details: [modding/tasks.md](../modding/tasks.md).
- **World reads and writes from a task: `WorldConcurrent` (Kiln.World), experimental under KILNX003.** A task reads entities and components (`Open`, `Read`, `ReadMany`, `Query`) and changes a component with a compare-exchange (`TryCompareExchange`), which Kiln makes under the engine's own write gate between frames. They work on the two game builds Kiln has exact profiles for (server 26430253, client 26430625). On any other build every read and write answers `Unavailable`, and the capability rows `world.ecs_read` and `world.ecs_write` say why. These members are new and marked `[Experimental("KILNX003")]`, which the compiler reports as an error until you opt in: `<NoWarn>$(NoWarn);KILNX003</NoWarn>` in the mod's project, and in its test project when the tests call them. Pre-release builds of the task branch marked them `KILNX001`, which no longer opts in to them. Details: [services/world.md](../services/world.md#world-reads-from-task-threads), [modding/analyzers.md](../modding/analyzers.md#kilnx003).
- **Tasks in unit tests (Kiln.TestKit).** A mod's tasks run on the kit's own pool. `kiln.Tasks.Settle()` waits for them and steps the server while they wait for the tick, and `kiln.Tasks.Live` counts the tasks still running. `KilnHarnessOptions.Side` picks a dedicated server, a hosting client or a joined client, with `EnterWorld`, `LeaveWorld` and `OnMainThread`. `FakeWorld.BusyReads` and `BusyWrites` (KILNX003) make the next reads or writes answer busy. Details: [modding/unit-tests.md](../modding/unit-tests.md).
- **New analyzer warnings in existing code.** KILN105 now also reports blocking waits (`Wait()`, `.Result`, `GetAwaiter().GetResult()`, `Thread.Sleep`) in `[Command]` methods, `Commands.Register` and `Executes` handlers and `Chat.Received` handlers, because commands run on the server tick and a blocking wait there freezes the server. KILN101 and KILN105 also follow Kiln tasks. Warnings only: a build stops only if the mod raised their severity. Details: [modding/analyzers.md](../modding/analyzers.md).
- **Kiln's own steps at the game's exit.** At a normal exit Kiln now cancels queued tasks, signals running ones, closes the engine's write gate and parks the task pool, waiting up to 500 ms for bodies still running. A task's `done` is not guaranteed at exit. A native plugin frees what it must in `on_unload`, which on native Windows runs only with `"unloadAtExit": "on"`. On native Windows Kiln runs these steps by default from this release on, and a plugin's `on_unload` there stays off unless `kiln.json` has `"unloadAtExit": "on"`. Under Wine and Proton the plugins' `on_unload` handlers now run after the pool has parked. Details: [services/tasks.md](../services/tasks.md#exit).
- **`kiln/state/graphics.json`** (Kiln.Ui, client): what Kiln's graphics tools do, the upscaler behind the game's FSR 3 above all: the choice, what runs, the reason code when the choice does not run, the version, the vendor files Kiln found. Written when it changes, never every frame. The launcher reads it. Schema: `schema/state-graphics.schema.json`, reason codes: [graphics.md](../graphics.md#what-runs-now-and-why-not).
- **Wraps in the prediction storage** (mod API 0.2.10): `WrapOptions.World = WorldKind.Prediction`, capability `systems.wraps.prediction` (`Systems.PredictionCapability`, on where `systems.wraps.client` is). The player's own client runs the local character's server programs again in its prediction storage (`equipment_visuals`, which sets the stance, among them), so a server wrap that changes what that player's own character does needs the same stage there to show on that player's screen. Before and After stages (per slice or per entity, typed views too) on a program of the client world's table that the prediction storage steps, and no Replace. Native: `game_program_wrap` takes `KILN_WORLD_PREDICTION` with the client directory's index. Where the capability is off it stays the `ArgumentException` / `KILN_ERR_INVALID_ARGUMENT` it was. `Systems.Find(name, WorldKind.Client)` lists them with the program's client wraps. Make one while a client world runs, on Kiln's tick thread (on a host the server world's, not `Client.Tick`): in the main menu `Wrap` answers `Ok = false`. Not yet run in a real game. Details: [services/systems.md](../services/systems.md#the-prediction-storage-worldkindprediction-since-020-dev2918).
- **`EcsProgramContext.TryReadWritable` / `TryGetWritable`** (mod API 0.2.10): read a component the program declares under `write`, before or after writing it, without a second `read` or `otherRead` entry. Details: [modding/ecs-programs.md](../modding/ecs-programs.md).
- **Client-world programs, checked for their first real use.** For a client program that changes how the game draws an entity it did not make (a weapon on a character), the client build was checked statically. The path resolves and runs for every client world. A client program runs after the game's programs it conflicts with: a `SlotAttachment` write is drawn from the next client step, and a client-world `BeforeEach` wrap on `slot_attachment` acts in the same step. Entities the server sends keep the server's ids in a client's world, so a player's character id from the server names that character on every client. `tpl` is proven for template components such as `ModelResource`. Kiln.Game's `Ecs.SlotAttachment`, `Ecs.EquipmentAnimationSets` and their enums are the client's layouts. What only a real client shows is listed. Details: [services/world.md](../services/world.md#a-client-program-on-the-games-entities-what-holds-020-dev2918).
- **kiln.ui after its overlay turned off during a session** (native plugins): `KilnUiStatus.error` gives the reason, frame and pre-overlay callbacks are not called any more, `wheel_open` returns `KILN_ERR_UNAVAILABLE`, and the rows `ui.windows`, `ui.draw`, `ui.menu_page`, `render.draw` and `kiln.screen` turn unavailable with the same text. Pre-overlay callbacks are only called for a present on a queue the game got itself and whose family can draw, and `queue_family` is that queue's own. No table change. Details: [services/ui.md](../services/ui.md).
- **The support report's `report.json` has a `versions` object**: `{kiln, launcher, os, runner, gameBuild, modsOn, deck}` (Kiln's version in the game, the launcher's, the system, the Proton or Wine build or Windows, the game's Steam build, how many mods are on, a Steam Deck). It is for tools that read reports, and Kiln support's posts take their versions line from it. It names no mod. It is added, and nothing else in the file changes.
- **`graphics_status` in kiln_ui.h** (native plugins): what Kiln's frame cap, DLSS settings and readout do now (the cap, frames rendered and shown a second, the DLSS in use with its mode, preset, render and output size, and what the tools step aside for). Appended after `set_texts` with `graphics_set` (Kiln.Ui's only: a native plugin's call is refused). Mods have no managed call for it in this release. Details: [graphics.md](../graphics.md#for-native-plugins-kiln_uih), [services/ui.md](../services/ui.md#kilns-graphics-tools-020-dev2918).
- **The upscaler's status in `graphics_status`.** The struct grows from 72 to 248 bytes: which upscaler runs behind the game's FSR 3 option, a stable reason code when the chosen one does not, flags, what it steps aside for, its version, the pass's sizes and a short English detail. A 72-byte caller still gets the fields it knows. Details: [graphics.md](../graphics.md#for-native-plugins-kiln_uih).
